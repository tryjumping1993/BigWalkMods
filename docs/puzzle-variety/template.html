The rework

Your rules of 24 November: sealed rooms should be seldom, most puzzles should be for three or four players working together, some should be within reach of one or two, and far fewer should lean on a timer. This is the plan for each puzzle, and how far it has got: all 24 that were to change are built and in the release of 24 November. Each was tried in the game by one test player (it starts, stops and wins, and most were solved by their own rules); none has been played by two or more people yet, and no real throw or ski run has been made. Which five relays stay, and each new design, are my choices for you to overrule. The sections after this one describe the puzzles as they were before the rework; a card marked Reworked shows that puzzle as it is now, with a picture taken after the rework.

Before the rework, in short

  • One kind of puzzle, 29 times.In every puzzle the answer is shown to one player who cannot act on it, the acting is done by a player who cannot see it, and the work is getting it across. None asks the group to work an answer out, explore, or build something.
  • Two engines underneath.{{n_pio}} follow the SDK's Press In Order: a lamp picks a target, someone calls it, and a player must be on it, through it or holding it when time is up. The other {{n_board}} run on Display and Peg Board: pictures or tunes are described and the matching tiles are hung.
  • The same three walls.{{n:hut}} of 29 show the cue only inside a closed hut. {{n:quiet}} also restrict voices (one-way lines, soundproofing, radio dead zones). {{n:middle}} pass the answer through a middle player who retells it.
  • Every failure costs everything.In all 29 a failed attempt starts again from nothing, with new pictures where it has any; four allow two or three wrong checks first. {{n:streak}} are streaks where one miss resets the count. Every design was written to be harder than the base game's nearest puzzle, and none is short.
  • The variety is in the body and the channel.Skis, snowballs, mazes, blindfolds, invisible ink, humming and mime change what it feels like. Seven puzzles break the pattern in some real way; they are named further down.

The most alike

Seventeen of the 29 fall into eight groups built on the same idea. The figure is how many of this page's traits the two share, out of all the traits either has. Where it is low, the place and the jobs are the same and the rules round them differ.

  • {{pair:AdventTrail:RadioSilence}}traits shared
    Advent Trail and Radio Silence
    Three pictures in one sealed room and two in the next, passed down a one-way microphone-to-headphones line, merged into order by the middle player, hung from a rack of scribbles against a clock. Radio Silence seals all three rooms and adds a lamp button back up the line. They stand about 150 m apart in the Plaza hub. Stocking Sort is close to both ({{pair:AdventTrail:StockingSort}}).
  • {{pair:Footprints:ThinIce}}traits shared
    Footprints and Thin Ice
    A lamp board in a soundproof hut, heard in the headphones of a raised seat, called on by voice to a walker on a grid, ten in a row. Thin Ice blindfolds the walker and cuts holes in the grid; Footprints hides the pads.
  • {{pair:HoldTheStar:MidnightFinale}}traits shared
    Hold the Star and Midnight Finale
    A caller in a soundproof room, a one-way push-to-talk line, stations of three hold buttons, 5 s rounds, a streak, then everyone acting at once. Midnight Finale calls scribbles that change places; Hold the Star calls colours.
  • {{pair:BlizzardWalk:WhiteoutMaze}}traits shared
    Blizzard Walk and Whiteout Maze
    A snow maze, a reader in a hut who cannot see it, a guide high above who can, and a walker sent to dead ends. Whiteout Maze blindfolds the walker and names the dead ends by picture; Blizzard Walk puts the reader 300 m away and times every phase.
  • {{pair:Downhill:Slalom}}traits shared
    Downhill and Slalom
    Both are called "the Called Slalom": six rows of three coloured gates, with the live one shown only on a lamp map in a timing hut. Downhill has one skier, two callers and a ball to deliver; Slalom needs two skiers in the lane together.
  • {{pair:SnowballRange:SummitShot}}traits shared
    Snowball Range and Summit Shot
    A caller with lamps beside pictures, a spotter who can see the targets, and a thrower who cannot. Summit Shot adds the blindfold and the wall and makes misses free; the Range rations the snowballs.
  • {{pair:BlindCarols:WhisperingChimney}}traits shared
    Blind Carols and Whispering Chimney
    A tune heard only in a headphone chair, hummed to a go-between, matched among the same nine speaking tiles. Blind Carols has blind hands and a clock; Whispering Chimney has neither, and lets the matcher hum back.
  • {{pair:BaubleHunt:SnowBridge}}traits shared
    Bauble Hunt, Blind Sculptor and Snow Bridge
    The three invisible-ink puzzles: one player reads a display, one sees the tiles' pictures through the ink telescope, and the hands see only blanks. Bauble Hunt fetches and posts, Blind Sculptor blindfolds the hands, Snow Bridge has two boards.

The ones that break the pattern

  • Light the Tree. No caller and nobody shut in: all three players watch a neighbour's lamps and press their own.
  • Reindeer Count. Reversed: the spotters are outside and many, the doer is the one shut in, and the first question is who can see the lamp at all.
  • Sleigh Run. The player carrying the load is also the one describing; the others open doors for them.
  • Glass Booth. The first link has no words, only pointing.
  • Lookouts. The only one that uses the island's size: its players are up to 1.4 km apart and talk by radio.
  • Whispering Chimney and Snow Bridge. With Bauble Hunt, the only three with no clock and no streak (a wrong check still ends the attempt). Whispering Chimney is also the one design in which the answer is hummed back to be checked.

What no puzzle asks for

  • Working something out. No deduction, counting, pattern, riddle or code to crack. The answer is always on show to somebody.
  • Exploring or travelling. Bauble Hunt's search stays inside one fenced wood, and only Lookouts sends anyone across the island. The railway, the mine, the planes, the gondola, the map, the compass and the GPS are never part of a puzzle.
  • Building or balancing. Snowballs are carried and thrown; nothing is stacked, bridged or assembled.
  • The whole group together. Every puzzle parts the group: somebody is always sent off to a hut, a tower or a booth. Pairs work together in a few (Slalom's two skiers, Snowman's builders, the runners in Fairy Lights), never everyone.
  • A gentle one. Every puzzle ends the attempt on a failure. Four allow two or three wrong checks first, and Summit Shot lets a throw miss for free. The shortest guess is 8 to 15 minutes.
  • The island's own weather and night. None uses the hour, the dark, a blizzard, the sea, or the dinosaurs.

The puzzles

Where this comes from. Each puzzle's design spec and build notes from the redesign of 22 November, checked against the puzzle code in release 2026.11.24. On 22 November each of the 29 started, stopped and won by test command in the running game. None has been played by a group, so the times are the designs' own guesses for a first solve by three players, and nothing here says how any of them feels to play.

How the traits were tagged. By reading the design notes. "Voices restricted" is counted only where the design guards sound itself (soundproofing, a one-way line, or a radio dead zone); where a puzzle is split by sight alone and talk is free, it is not.

Pictures. Each site from the south at eye height, captured in the game on 22 November, before the lowland's trees were thinned and the lights were changed. The small mark in the middle is the game's reticle.
